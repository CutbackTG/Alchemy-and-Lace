Cloth with a past

The history within kimono fabric

Kimono textiles carry more than pattern and colour. They preserve traces of changing taste, craft traditions, ceremony, family life, and the hands that made and wore them.

From robe to textile archive

The kimono developed through centuries of Japanese dress history. Its apparently simple construction created a broad surface for weaving, dyeing, embroidery, resist work, and symbolic pattern. Motifs could evoke seasons, plants, animals, landscapes, good fortune, status, or personal identity.

Silk, pattern, and skilled hands

Many surviving kimono fabrics were produced through painstaking processes. Woven brocades, hand-dyed silk, shibori resist techniques, yuzen decoration, metallic thread, and embroidery transformed cloth into something closer to a portable artwork.

Reclamation rather than erasure

Anarchy & Lace works with reclaimed Japanese textiles, allowing damaged, unworn, or fragmented cloth to continue in a new form. The aim is not to disguise age, but to honour it. Small irregularities, repairs, fading, and variation are part of the material's biography.

A new garment, an older story

Each finished piece joins contemporary design with inherited fabric. No two lengths of reclaimed cloth behave identically, so every garment emerges as a singular collaboration between past craft and present construction.
